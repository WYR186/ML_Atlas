PAC Learnability and ERM

Lecture 22 introduces the PAC framework: a way to say, mathematically, that learning from samples should work with high probability and small error, without assuming a friendly data distribution.

1. Concept Understanding

PAC stands for Probably Approximately Correct. "Probably" means confidence at least $1-\delta$ over the random training set. "Approximately correct" means test error at most $\epsilon$ or within $\epsilon$ of the best-in-class predictor.

ERM, empirical risk minimization, chooses a predictor with the smallest training error inside the hypothesis class.

  • In the realizable case, the target concept is assumed to belong to $\mathcal{F}$.
  • In the agnostic case, no perfect concept is assumed; the goal is to compete with the best function in $\mathcal{H}$.
  • Sample complexity is the number of examples needed as a function of $\epsilon$, $\delta$, and model complexity.

2. Plain-English Explanation

PAC is a promise with two knobs. Turn $\epsilon$ down and you demand a more accurate learner. Turn $\delta$ down and you demand more confidence. Both demands cost more samples.

ERM is the natural student strategy: among all allowed explanations, pick the one that makes the fewest mistakes on the homework. PAC theory asks when that homework performance actually predicts exam performance.

3. Core Intuition

For one fixed classifier, training error concentrates around true error. The hard part is that the learner chooses a classifier after seeing data. We therefore need a uniform guarantee over the whole class, usually paid for by a complexity term such as $\log|\mathcal{F}|$ or VC dimension.

This is why finite classes are easier: a union bound can control all hypotheses at once.

4. Key Equations

Training error
$$ \hat\epsilon_{\mathcal{D}}(f)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf{1}\{f(x^{(i)})\ne y^{(i)}\} $$

Symbols. The bullet list below defines each variable used in the formula.

  • $\mathcal{D}=\{(x^{(i)},y^{(i)})\}_{i=1}^n$ is the training set.
  • $n$ is the number of training examples.
  • $\mathbf{1}\{\cdot\}$ is an indicator: it equals 1 if the statement is true and 0 otherwise.
  • The hat in $\hat\epsilon$ means this error is measured from data.

How to read it. This is empirical 0-1 loss: each indicator records one training mistake, and the average turns the count into a rate.

How to use. count how many training points $f$ misclassifies, then divide by $n$.

Test error
$$ \epsilon_\mu(f)=\Pr_\mu(f(X)\ne Y) $$

Symbols. The bullet list below defines each variable used in the formula.

  • $\mu$ is the unknown data-generating distribution.
  • $(X,Y)\sim\mu$ is a fresh random test example, not necessarily one from training.
  • $\Pr_\mu$ asks for the probability of making a mistake under that distribution.

How to read it. This is population error on a fresh draw from the true distribution, so it is the quantity generalization theory tries to control.

How to use. this is the quantity PAC bounds try to control. It is usually not directly observable, so we estimate or upper-bound it from samples.

Finite realizable ERM bound
$$ n\ge \frac1\epsilon\left(\log|\mathcal{F}|+\log\frac1\delta\right) $$

Symbols. The bullet list below defines each variable used in the formula.

  • $n$ is the required sample size.
  • $\epsilon$ is the target error level: smaller $\epsilon$ means you demand a more accurate classifier.
  • $\delta$ is the allowed failure probability: smaller $\delta$ means you demand more confidence.
  • $|\mathcal{F}|$ is the number of hypotheses in the finite class.
  • $\log$ is typically natural log in these bounds; changing bases only changes constants.

How to read it. The inequality is a sample-complexity promise: enough samples make every bad finite-class hypothesis unlikely to fit the data perfectly.

How to use. choose target $\epsilon,\delta$, plug in $|\mathcal{F}|$, and solve for the minimum $n$. If $n$ meets this bound, realizable ERM has true error at most $\epsilon$ with probability at least $1-\delta$.

Agnostic PAC target
$$ \Pr\left(\epsilon_\mu(f)\le \min_{g\in\mathcal{H}}\epsilon_\mu(g)+\epsilon\right)\ge 1-\delta $$

Symbols. The bullet list below defines each variable used in the formula.

  • $\mathcal{H}$ is the hypothesis class used in the agnostic setting.
  • $\min_{g\in\mathcal{H}}\epsilon_\mu(g)$ is the best true error achievable inside $\mathcal{H}$.
  • The extra $+\epsilon$ is the allowed excess error over that best-in-class predictor.
  • The outer probability is over the random draw of the training set and any algorithmic randomness.

How to read it. The learner is compared with the best function in the class plus a tolerance, because noisy labels may make zero error impossible.

How to use. use this form when labels may be noisy or no perfect classifier is assumed. The learner is judged against the best function in the class, not against zero error.

How to apply these PAC formulas
Step 1: decide whether the problem is asking for empirical error, true error, or required sample size.
Step 2: if it asks for sample size, choose the target accuracy $\epsilon$ and failure probability $\delta$, then plug in $|\mathcal{F}|$.
Step 3: solve the inequality for $n$. Complexity costs $\log|\mathcal{F}|$, confidence costs $\log(1/\delta)$, and realizable accuracy costs roughly $1/\epsilon$.
Step 4: interpret the answer as a high-probability guarantee, not an exact prediction of test error.

5. Worked Examples

Example 1 · initial-segment learner

Source: Lecture 22, pp. 12-16.

The target labels points in $(0,1)$ by whether they lie before a threshold. If the learned threshold is wrong by at least $\epsilon$, an interval of length $\epsilon$ near the true threshold must contain no samples. That probability is at most $\exp(-n\epsilon)$ on one side, then a union bound handles both sides.

Example 2 · finite-class ERM

Source: Lecture 22, pp. 17-19.

A bad classifier with true error above $\epsilon$ has probability at most $(1-\epsilon)^n$ of making zero training mistakes. There are $|\mathcal{F}|$ possible classifiers, so the probability that any bad one fools ERM is at most $|\mathcal{F}|e^{-n\epsilon}$.

Example 3 · uniform convergence implies good ERM

Source: HW5, Section 1.1.

If every empirical risk is within $\epsilon$ of its true risk, then ERM's true risk is within $2\epsilon$ of the best true risk in the class: one $\epsilon$ for the learned model, one $\epsilon$ for the best-in-class model.

6. Problem-Solving Tips

  1. Identify whether the question is realizable or agnostic.
  2. For finite $\mathcal{F}$, expect a union bound over hypotheses.
  3. For fixed $f$, Hoeffding is enough; for data-chosen $\hat f$, use uniform convergence.
  4. When solving for sample size, set the failure probability bound $\le\delta$ and isolate $n$.
  5. Remember that smaller $\epsilon$ and smaller $\delta$ both increase sample complexity.

7. Common Mistakes

  • Applying a fixed-hypothesis Hoeffding bound directly to $\hat f$ chosen from the same data.
  • Forgetting the union bound factor $|\mathcal{F}|$.
  • Mixing up $\epsilon$ and $\delta$: $\epsilon$ is accuracy, $\delta$ is failure probability.
  • Saying PAC learnability includes runtime by definition; Lecture 22's definition focuses on sample complexity.

8. Exam Focus

  • Define PAC learnability in words using $\epsilon$ and $\delta$.
  • Write ERM and explain empirical risk versus true risk.
  • Derive or recognize the finite-class sample complexity.
  • Explain why a data-dependent hypothesis needs uniform convergence.

9. Quick Checklist

  • PAC = high probability plus low error.
  • $\epsilon$ controls accuracy.
  • $\delta$ controls confidence.
  • ERM minimizes training error.
  • Finite class penalty is $\log|\mathcal{F}|$.

PAC Learnability 与 ERM

Lecture 22 引入 PAC framework：我们希望用样本学出来的 predictor，在高概率下测试误差小，而且不依赖某个特别友好的数据分布。

1. Concept Understanding · 概念理解

PAC 是 Probably Approximately Correct。“Probably” 指训练集随机性下成功概率至少 $1-\delta$；“Approximately correct” 指 test error 至多 $\epsilon$，或比 class 内最优多不超过 $\epsilon$。

ERM 是 empirical risk minimization：在 hypothesis class 里选 training error 最小的 predictor。

  • realizable case：目标 concept 假设真的属于 $\mathcal{F}$。
  • agnostic case：不假设完美函数存在，只要求接近 $\mathcal{H}$ 内最优。
  • sample complexity：为了达到 $\epsilon,\delta$ 需要多少样本。

2. Plain-English Explanation · 大白话理解

PAC 像一个带两个旋钮的承诺：$\epsilon$ 越小，你要求答案越准；$\delta$ 越小，你要求翻车概率越低。两个旋钮拧紧都会增加样本需求。

ERM 就像学生在允许的所有解释里，选一个最能解释作业题的答案。PAC theory 问的是：作业做得好，什么时候能说明考试也会好？

3. Core Intuition · 核心直觉

对固定 classifier，training error 会集中到 true error 附近。难点是 learner 会看完数据再挑 $\hat f$，所以需要对整个 class 同时成立的 uniform guarantee。

有限 hypothesis class 比较好处理，因为可以用 union bound，把每个 hypothesis 的坏事件加起来。

4. Key Equations · 关键公式

Training error
$$ \hat\epsilon_{\mathcal{D}}(f)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf{1}\{f(x^{(i)})\ne y^{(i)}\} $$

符号. 下面的 bullet list 逐项解释公式里的变量。

  • $\mathcal{D}=\{(x^{(i)},y^{(i)})\}_{i=1}^n$ 是 training set。
  • $n$ 是训练样本数量。
  • $\mathbf{1}\{\cdot\}$ 是 indicator：括号里的事件为真就等于 1，否则等于 0。
  • $\hat\epsilon$ 上面的 hat 表示这是从数据上量出来的 empirical error。

怎么理解. 把公式先当成“输入哪些量、输出哪个量”的计算规则来看；符号含义确定后，再按步骤代入。

怎么用.数一数 $f$ 在训练集上错了多少个点，再除以 $n$。

Test error
$$ \epsilon_\mu(f)=\Pr_\mu(f(X)\ne Y) $$

符号. 下面的 bullet list 逐项解释公式里的变量。

  • $\mu$ 是未知的数据生成分布。
  • $(X,Y)\sim\mu$ 是一个新的 random test example，不是训练集里的某个固定点。
  • $\Pr_\mu$ 表示在真实分布下发生分类错误的概率。

怎么理解. 把公式先当成“输入哪些量、输出哪个量”的计算规则来看；符号含义确定后，再按步骤代入。

怎么用.PAC bound 真正想控制的是这个量。它通常不能直接算，只能用样本估计或给 upper bound。

有限 realizable ERM bound
$$ n\ge \frac1\epsilon\left(\log|\mathcal{F}|+\log\frac1\delta\right) $$

符号. 下面的 bullet list 逐项解释公式里的变量。

  • $n$ 是需要的 sample size。
  • $\epsilon$ 是目标错误率，越小代表要求越准。
  • $\delta$ 是允许失败的概率，越小代表要求越有把握。
  • $|\mathcal{F}|$ 是 finite hypothesis class 里的 hypothesis 数量。
  • $\log$ 通常按 natural log 理解；底数不同只会改变常数。

怎么理解. 把公式先当成“输入哪些量、输出哪个量”的计算规则来看；符号含义确定后，再按步骤代入。

怎么用.给定目标 $\epsilon,\delta$ 和 $|\mathcal{F}|$，代入右边求最小 $n$。满足这个样本数时，realizable ERM 以至少 $1-\delta$ 概率得到 true error $\le\epsilon$。

Agnostic PAC 目标
$$ \Pr\left(\epsilon_\mu(f)\le \min_{g\in\mathcal{H}}\epsilon_\mu(g)+\epsilon\right)\ge 1-\delta $$

符号. 下面的 bullet list 逐项解释公式里的变量。

  • $\mathcal{H}$ 是 agnostic setting 里的 hypothesis class。
  • $\min_{g\in\mathcal{H}}\epsilon_\mu(g)$ 是 $\mathcal{H}$ 内能达到的最小 true error。
  • $+\epsilon$ 是允许比 class 内最优多出来的 excess error。
  • 外面的 probability 来自随机抽到的 training set，以及算法本身可能的 randomness。

怎么理解. 把公式先当成“输入哪些量、输出哪个量”的计算规则来看；符号含义确定后，再按步骤代入。

怎么用.当 label 有噪声、或者不假设存在完美 classifier 时，用这个形式。此时不是要求 error 接近 0，而是接近 class 内最优。

这些 PAC 公式怎么套题
Step 1: 先判断题目要 empirical error、true error，还是 required sample size。
Step 2: 如果要求 sample size，先定目标 accuracy $\epsilon$ 和 failure probability $\delta$，再代入 $|\mathcal{F}|$。
Step 3: 解不等式得到 $n$。复杂度代价是 $\log|\mathcal{F}|$，confidence 代价是 $\log(1/\delta)$，realizable accuracy 代价大约是 $1/\epsilon$。
Step 4: 最后把结果理解成 high-probability guarantee，不是 test error 的精确预测。

5. Worked Examples · 代表性例题

例 1 · initial-segment learner

出处：Lecture 22, pp. 12-16。

目标是在 $(0,1)$ 上按 threshold 标记。若学到的 threshold 偏差至少 $\epsilon$，真 threshold 附近长度为 $\epsilon$ 的区间必须没有 sample。一侧概率至多 $\exp(-n\epsilon)$，两侧再用 union bound。

例 2 · finite-class ERM

出处：Lecture 22, pp. 17-19。

真实错误率超过 $\epsilon$ 的坏 classifier，在 $n$ 个样本上零训练错误的概率最多是 $(1-\epsilon)^n$。一共有 $|\mathcal{F}|$ 个 classifier，所以任意坏 classifier 骗过 ERM 的概率最多 $|\mathcal{F}|e^{-n\epsilon}$。

例 3 · uniform convergence 推 ERM

出处：HW5, Section 1.1。

如果每个 empirical risk 和 true risk 都相差不超过 $\epsilon$，那么 ERM 的 true risk 距离 class 内最优 true risk 不超过 $2\epsilon$：一个 $\epsilon$ 控制学出的模型，一个 $\epsilon$ 控制 class 内最优模型。

6. Problem-Solving Tips · 解题技巧

  1. 先判断是 realizable 还是 agnostic。
  2. 有限 $\mathcal{F}$ 通常要对 hypotheses 做 union bound。
  3. 固定 $f$ 用 Hoeffding；数据选出的 $\hat f$ 要用 uniform convergence。
  4. 求 sample size 时，把 failure probability bound 设成 $\le\delta$ 再解 $n$。
  5. $\epsilon$ 越小、$\delta$ 越小，sample complexity 都会上升。

7. Common Mistakes · 常见错误

  • 把 fixed-hypothesis Hoeffding bound 直接套到同一数据选出的 $\hat f$。
  • 忘记 union bound 带来的 $|\mathcal{F}|$ 因子。
  • 混淆 $\epsilon$ 和 $\delta$：$\epsilon$ 是 accuracy，$\delta$ 是失败概率。
  • 说 PAC learnability 定义里一定包含运行时间；Lecture 22 重点是 sample complexity。

8. Exam Focus · 考试重点

  • 会用 $\epsilon,\delta$ 口头定义 PAC learnability。
  • 会写 ERM，并区分 empirical risk 与 true risk。
  • 会推或识别 finite-class sample complexity。
  • 会解释为什么 data-dependent hypothesis 需要 uniform convergence。

9. Quick Checklist · 速记清单

  • PAC = high probability + low error。
  • $\epsilon$ 控制 accuracy。
  • $\delta$ 控制 confidence / failure probability。
  • ERM 最小化 training error。
  • 有限类复杂度惩罚是 $\log|\mathcal{F}|$。
