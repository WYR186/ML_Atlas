🎲 Probability

1. Concept Understanding

Formal definitions and the problem they solve

A random variable $X$ assigns a number to each outcome of a random experiment, and its distribution $P(X=x)$ tells you how often each value occurs. Two variables also have a joint distribution $P(X_1, X_2)$ over all pairs.

From there, four ideas matter for ML:

  • Conditional probability: $P(A\mid B)=P(A,B)/P(B)$ — the probability of $A$ once we know $B$ happened.
  • Independence: $P(A,B)=P(A)P(B)$ — knowing one does not change the other.
  • Conditional independence: $P(A,B\mid C)=P(A\mid C)\,P(B\mid C)$ — the apparent link between $A$ and $B$ disappears once $C$ is known.
  • Bayes' rule: lets us flip the direction of conditioning, turning $P(B\mid A)$ into $P(A\mid B)$ via the prior.

Why ML needs this: every classifier silently asks "given features $X_1,\dots,X_d$, which label $Y$ is most likely?" — i.e. compute $P(Y\mid X)$. Bayes' rule rewrites that in terms of the class prior $P(Y)$ and class-conditional likelihood $P(X\mid Y)$, both estimable from data.

2. Plain-English Explanation

Without formulas first

Conditioning means narrowing the world: "given that $B$ already happened, how often does $A$ also happen?" You first slice the pie down to the part where $B$ holds, then count.

Independence is "the two events have nothing to do with each other" — observing one tells you nothing about the other.

Conditional independence is the subtler one. Two umbrellas opening at the same time look correlated, but once you condition on "it is raining," that link vanishes — both were just reacting to the rain.

Bayes' rule is rational belief updating: start with a prior, look at the evidence, and rescale. posterior $\propto$ likelihood × prior.

3. Core Intuition

Why these ideas actually work

Conditioning shrinks the sample space. $P(A\mid B)$ literally throws away outcomes where $B$ failed and renormalizes by the area of the remaining "new world," $P(B)$.

Bayes' rule is just algebra. Multiply $P(A\mid B)P(B)=P(A,B)=P(B\mid A)P(A)$ and divide by $P(B)$ — two seconds of work, but flipping causes and effects unlocks an enormous amount of inference.

Conditional independence is how ML escapes the curse of dimensionality. With $d$ binary features, the joint $P(X_1,\dots,X_d\mid Y)$ has $\sim 2^d$ free parameters. Assume conditional independence and it collapses to $2d$. Naive Bayes lives or dies by this single trick (Lecture 2, p. 18–22).

Where this shows up later. Posterior $\propto$ likelihood × prior reappears in MAP estimation, the Bayes classifier, MLE for Naive Bayes, Gaussian NB (HW1 §2), VAE / diffusion priors, and Bellman expectation in RL.

4. Key Equations

Conditional Probability
$$ P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(A,B)}{P(B)},\qquad P(B)>0 $$

Symbols. $A, B$ are events. $P(A,B)$ is the joint probability that both occur. $P(B)$ is the marginal of the conditioning event (sum / integrate the joint over $A$). The formula is undefined when $P(B)=0$.

How to read it. Conditioning shrinks the world to outcomes where $B$ holds and renormalizes by the area of that "new world," $P(B)$. The denominator is the size of the slice you've kept.

How to use. Whenever a problem says "the probability of $A$ given $B$": look up the joint $P(A,B)$ in a table, divide by the marginal $P(B)$. The result is a probability in $[0,1]$.

Bayes' Rule
$$ P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(B\mid A)\,P(A)}{P(B)} $$

Symbols. $P(A)$ is the prior (your belief before seeing evidence). $P(B\mid A)$ is the likelihood (chance of observing $B$ when $A$ is true). $P(A\mid B)$ is the posterior (belief after seeing $B$). $P(B)$ is the normalizer that makes posteriors sum to 1.

How to read it. Rational belief update — start with a prior, rescale each hypothesis by how well it explains the evidence. Slogan: posterior $\propto$ likelihood × prior.

How to use. Use whenever you have $P(B\mid A)$ but want $P(A\mid B)$. Multiply prior × likelihood; if $P(B)$ is not given, expand it via the law of total probability ($P(B)=\sum_a P(B\mid A=a)P(A=a)$).

Independence
$$ P(A,B)=P(A)P(B)\;\Leftrightarrow\;P(A\mid B)=P(A) $$

Symbols. $A, B$ are events. The two forms (joint factorization and conditional equality) are mathematically equivalent — pick whichever is easier to verify.

How to read it. Knowing $B$ tells you nothing new about $A$; the joint distribution factorizes into the product of marginals.

How to use. To test, compute both sides on a small contingency table and compare. To use as an assumption (e.g. dice rolls), substitute $P(A,B)\to P(A)P(B)$ to simplify joint probabilities into products.

Conditional Independence
$$ P(A,B\mid C)=P(A\mid C)\,P(B\mid C) $$

Symbols. $C$ is the conditioning variable (in Naive Bayes, the class label $Y$). $A, B$ are the two events whose link you're testing. Equivalent form: $P(A\mid B,C)=P(A\mid C)$.

How to read it. $A$ and $B$ may look correlated overall, but once $C$ is known, the link disappears. Two umbrellas opening together look correlated — until you condition on "it's raining" ($C$), at which point both events just react to $C$.

How to use. In NB or graphical-model problems, plug it in to factorize a high-dimensional joint $P(X_1,\dots,X_d\mid Y)$ as the product $\prod_i P(X_i\mid Y)$. Always verify the assumption is reasonable for the data.

Law of Total Probability
$$ P(B)=\sum_{a}P(B\mid A=a)\,P(A=a) $$

Symbols. $\{A=a\}$ is a partition of the sample space into mutually exclusive cases. $P(A=a)$ is the weight of each case (must sum to 1). $P(B\mid A=a)$ is the probability of $B$ inside that case.

How to read it. Split the world into cases of $A$, weight each case's probability of $B$ by how often that case occurs, and add them up.

How to use. Use whenever you need $P(B)$ but only have the conditionals — the most common use is filling in the denominator of Bayes' rule.

Expectation & Variance
$$ \mathbb{E}[X]=\sum_x x\,P(x),\quad \mathrm{Var}(X)=\mathbb{E}[X^2]-\mathbb{E}[X]^2 $$

Symbols. $X$ is a random variable. $\mathbb{E}[X]$ is the mean. $\mathrm{Var}(X)$ is the variance (a measure of spread). Square root of variance is the standard deviation.

How to read it. Expectation = long-run average outcome. Variance = how far typical outcomes deviate from that average (squared, so units are unit-of-$X$ squared).

How to use. Plug each value $x$ and its probability $P(x)$ into the sum. Continuous case: replace $\sum_x x\,P(x)$ with $\int x\,p(x)\,dx$ against the density $p$. Variance shortcut: $\mathbb{E}[X^2]-(\mathbb{E}[X])^2$ is usually faster than $\mathbb{E}[(X-\mu)^2]$.

Naive Bayes Factorization (preview)
$$ P(Y\mid X_1,\dots,X_d) \;\propto\; P(Y)\prod_{i=1}^{d} P(X_i\mid Y) $$

Symbols. $Y$ is the class label. $X_i$ is feature $i$ (out of $d$). $P(Y)$ is the class prior (how common each class is). $P(X_i\mid Y)$ is the per-feature class-conditional likelihood. The "$\propto$" hides the normalizer $P(X)$, which doesn't depend on $y$ and so doesn't affect the $\arg\max$.

How to read it. Bayes' rule supplies the numerator; the conditional-independence assumption lets you factorize the likelihood as a product of one-feature pieces. This drops the number of parameters from $\sim 2^d$ down to $2d$ — the only reason NB is tractable in high dimensions.

How to use. Train by counting feature occurrences within each class to estimate $P(Y)$ and each $P(X_i\mid Y)$. Predict with $\hat y=\arg\max_y P(Y)\prod_i P(X_i\mid Y)$. In code, use $\log$ sums instead of products to avoid floating-point underflow.

5. Worked Examples

Example 1 · Two dice and conditioning (Lecture 2, p. 5–6)

Roll two fair six-sided dice with values $X_1, X_2$. Find $P(X_1=2 \mid X_1+X_2\le 4)$.

Outcomes with $X_1+X_2\le 4$: $(1,1),(1,2),(1,3),(2,1),(2,2),(3,1)$ — six total. Among them, $X_1=2$ occurs in $(2,1)$ and $(2,2)$.

$$ P(X_1=2 \mid X_1+X_2\le 4)=\frac{2}{6}=\frac{1}{3}. $$
Example 2 · Bayes calculation

Given $P(A)=0.3,\ P(B\mid A)=0.8,\ P(B\mid \neg A)=0.2$, find $P(A\mid B)$.

$$ P(B)=0.8(0.3)+0.2(0.7)=0.38 $$
$$ P(A\mid B)=\frac{0.8\cdot 0.3}{0.38}\approx 0.6316 $$

Reading: prior on $A$ was 30%, but observing $B$ raised it to ~63% because $B$ is much more likely under $A$ than under $\neg A$.

Example 3 · Conditional independence trap (Lecture 2, p. 9–10)

Two dice. Let $A=\{X_1\text{ odd}\}$, $B=\{X_2\text{ odd}\}$, $S=\{X_1+X_2\text{ even}\}$. Unconditionally $A\perp B$ (each $\tfrac12$, joint $\tfrac14$).

But given $S$, they become dependent: if $A$ holds, then $B$ must hold too for the sum to be even, so $P(B\mid A,S)=1\ne P(B\mid S)=\tfrac12$.

Lesson: independence does not imply conditional independence (and vice versa). Always check the definition.

Example 4 · Estimating from a contingency table (Lecture 2, p. 14–17)

From the lung-cancer toy table, estimate $P(\text{Cancer}=Y\mid \text{Gender}=M,\ \text{Smoke}=Y)$. Rows with $M,Y,Y$: 1000. Rows with $M,Y$ regardless of cancer: $1000+5000=6000$. So $\hat P=1000/6000\approx 16.7\%$.

This works because the table is small. With 100 binary features the table would have $2^{100}\approx 10^{30}$ rows — far more than people on Earth — and most cells would be empty. That is the curse of dimensionality (Lecture 2 p. 17–18).

6. Problem-Solving Tips

How to attack a probability question
  1. Spot the question type. "A and B" → joint, factor with chain rule. "A given B" → conditional, use Bayes if you only have $P(B\mid A)$. "Independent?" → check $P(A,B)=P(A)P(B)$.
  2. If $P(B)$ is missing, expand it with the law of total probability.
  3. For "$A\perp B$ given $C$?", compute $P(A\mid B,C)$ and $P(A\mid C)$ on a tiny table — equal iff conditionally independent.
  4. For ML classification, write $P(Y\mid X)\propto P(Y)\prod_i P(X_i\mid Y)$ and $\arg\max_y$.
  5. Sanity check: try extreme priors (e.g. $P(B\mid A)=1$, $P(B\mid \neg A)=0$); if your formula does not give the obvious answer, you dropped a term.

7. Common Mistakes

Easy ways to lose points
  • Wrong denominator: $P(A\mid B)=P(A,B)/P(B)$ — never $P(A)$.
  • Confusing $P(A\mid B)$ with $P(B\mid A)$ ("prosecutor's fallacy"); they are linked only through priors.
  • Treating independence and conditional independence as the same thing — Example 3 is the textbook counterexample.
  • Dropping the prior in Bayes (base-rate neglect).
  • Using "$\propto$" but forgetting to normalize at the end.
  • Multiplying conditionals when variables are not conditionally independent — the NB factorization is an assumption, not a free identity.

8. Exam Focus

What you must walk in able to do
  • Write the five core formulas (conditional, Bayes, independence, conditional independence, total probability) from memory in 30 seconds.
  • Compute $P(A\mid B)$ by hand from $P(A), P(B\mid A), P(B\mid \neg A)$, showing the total-probability expansion.
  • Recognize conditional independence in a graphical / NB problem and write the factorization.
  • Map "posterior $\propto$ likelihood × prior" onto the variables in a word problem.
  • Argue why a probability table fails in high dimensions ($2^d$ growth).

9. Quick Checklist

Last-minute recall

  • $P(A\mid B)=P(A,B)/P(B)$ — denominator is the conditioning event.
  • Bayes: $P(A\mid B)=P(B\mid A)P(A)/P(B)$.
  • $P(B)=\sum_a P(B\mid A=a)P(A=a)$ when missing.
  • Independence: $P(A,B)=P(A)P(B)$. CI: $P(A,B\mid C)=P(A\mid C)P(B\mid C)$.
  • Independence ↮ conditional independence.
  • Naive Bayes: $P(Y\mid X)\propto P(Y)\prod_i P(X_i\mid Y)$ — $2^d$ → $2d$ params.
  • Probabilities in $[0,1]$, conditionals sum to 1 over the conditioned variable.

🎲 概率论

1. 概念理解

正式定义与它解决的问题

随机变量 $X$ 给随机试验的每个结果赋一个数值，分布 $P(X=x)$ 描述每个值出现的频率；两个变量还有联合分布 $P(X_1, X_2)$，记录所有取值组合。

从这里出发，ML 真正用到的核心只有四件事：

  • 条件概率：$P(A\mid B)=P(A,B)/P(B)$，已知 $B$ 发生时 $A$ 的概率。
  • 独立：$P(A,B)=P(A)P(B)$，知道其中一个不会改变另一个。
  • 条件独立：$P(A,B\mid C)=P(A\mid C)\,P(B\mid C)$，给定 $C$ 后 $A$、$B$ 的关联消失。
  • 贝叶斯：把 $P(B\mid A)$ 翻转成 $P(A\mid B)$，借助先验。

ML 为什么需要它：每个分类器其实都在问“给定特征 $X_1,\dots,X_d$，最可能的标签 $Y$ 是什么？”——也就是 $P(Y\mid X)$。贝叶斯把它改写成类先验 $P(Y)$ 与类条件似然 $P(X\mid Y)$，二者都可以从带标签数据中估出。

2. 大白话理解

不看公式先懂大概

条件化就是把世界缩小：“在 $B$ 已经发生的世界里，$A$ 占多少？”先切饼，再数。

独立意思是“两件事毫无关系”——知道一个对猜另一个完全没帮助。

条件独立更微妙：两个人同时打伞看上去相关，但只要告诉你“在下雨”，两件事的关联就消失了——它们都只是在响应同一件事。

贝叶斯就是“理性的信念更新”：先验 → 见证据 → 重缩放，posterior $\propto$ likelihood × prior。

3. 核心直觉

这些概念为什么真的有效

条件化是在缩样本空间。$P(A\mid B)$ 把 $B$ 不成立的结果丢掉，再用剩下“新世界”的面积 $P(B)$ 重新归一化。

贝叶斯就是两步代数：$P(A\mid B)P(B)=P(A,B)=P(B\mid A)P(A)$，除以 $P(B)$ 而已。但“倒推”这一能力解锁了大量推理。

条件独立是 ML 跳出维度灾难的唯一手段。$d$ 个二值特征下 $P(X_1,\dots,X_d\mid Y)$ 有 $\sim 2^d$ 个参数；假设条件独立后只剩 $2d$。Naive Bayes 全靠这一招（Lecture 2, p. 18–22）。

串联后面的内容：MAP、Bayes 分类器、Naive Bayes 的 MLE、Gaussian NB（HW1 §2）、VAE / 扩散先验、强化学习的 Bellman 期望，都在反复用 posterior $\propto$ likelihood × prior。

4. 关键公式

条件概率
$$ P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(A,B)}{P(B)},\qquad P(B)>0 $$

符号. $A, B$ 为事件；$P(A,B)$ 是二者同时发生的联合概率；$P(B)$ 是被条件事件的边缘概率（把联合对 $A$ 求和 / 积分得到）；$P(B)=0$ 时未定义。

怎么理解. 条件化 = 把世界缩小到 $B$ 成立的那部分，再用"新世界"的面积 $P(B)$ 重新归一化；分母就是你保留的那块切片的大小。

怎么用. 题目说"在 $B$ 已发生时 $A$ 的概率"——查表算出联合 $P(A,B)$，再除以边缘 $P(B)$；结果是落在 $[0,1]$ 的概率。

贝叶斯公式
$$ P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(B\mid A)\,P(A)}{P(B)} $$

符号. $P(A)$ 是 prior（看到证据前的信念）；$P(B\mid A)$ 是 likelihood（在 $A$ 下看到 $B$ 的概率）；$P(A\mid B)$ 是 posterior（看到 $B$ 后的信念）；$P(B)$ 是让 posterior 求和为 1 的归一化常数。

怎么理解. 理性的信念更新——从先验出发，按"假设解释证据的程度"重缩放。口诀：posterior $\propto$ likelihood × prior。

怎么用. 题目给 $P(B\mid A)$ 而要 $P(A\mid B)$ 时使用；$P(B)$ 没给就用全概率公式展开 $P(B)=\sum_a P(B\mid A=a)P(A=a)$ 补上。

独立
$$ P(A,B)=P(A)P(B)\;\Leftrightarrow\;P(A\mid B)=P(A) $$

符号. $A, B$ 为事件；联合因子分解与条件等式两种写法数学上等价，挑容易验证的那个用。

怎么理解. 知道 $B$ 对 $A$ 的概率没有任何新信息；联合分布拆成边缘的乘积。

怎么用. 验独立：在小列联表上算两边再比较；用作假设时（如掷骰子），把 $P(A,B)$ 直接换成 $P(A)P(B)$ 简化联合概率。

条件独立
$$ P(A,B\mid C)=P(A\mid C)\,P(B\mid C) $$

符号. $C$ 是被条件化的变量（在 Naive Bayes 里就是类别 $Y$）；$A, B$ 是要检验关系的两个事件。等价写法：$P(A\mid B,C)=P(A\mid C)$。

怎么理解. 表面上 $A, B$ 相关，但一旦知道 $C$ 关联消失。两人同时打伞看上去相关，告诉你"在下雨" $C$ 之后，两件事就只是分别在响应共同因 $C$。

怎么用. NB / 图模型题里直接代入，把高维联合 $P(X_1,\dots,X_d\mid Y)$ 拆成 $\prod_i P(X_i\mid Y)$。代入前要确认这个假设对数据合理。

全概率公式
$$ P(B)=\sum_{a}P(B\mid A=a)\,P(A=a) $$

符号. $\{A=a\}$ 是样本空间的互斥划分；$P(A=a)$ 是各案的权重（求和必须为 1）；$P(B\mid A=a)$ 是该案下 $B$ 的条件概率。

怎么理解. 按 $A$ 的取值分情形，每个情形里 $B$ 的发生概率乘以该情形的出现频率，再加权求和。

怎么用. 没给 $P(B)$ 但有条件概率时用——最常见的就是补贝叶斯公式的分母。

期望与方差
$$ \mathbb{E}[X]=\sum_x x\,P(x),\quad \mathrm{Var}(X)=\mathbb{E}[X^2]-\mathbb{E}[X]^2 $$

符号. $X$ 是随机变量；$\mathbb{E}[X]$ 是 mean（均值）；$\mathrm{Var}(X)$ 是 variance（离散程度）；标准差是方差的平方根。

怎么理解. 期望 = 长期平均；方差 = 典型取值偏离均值的程度（平方过，单位是 $X$ 的平方）。

怎么用. 把每个取值 $x$ 与其概率 $P(x)$ 代入求和；连续情形换成对密度 $p$ 的积分 $\int x\,p(x)\,dx$。方差的快捷算法 $\mathbb{E}[X^2]-(\mathbb{E}[X])^2$ 通常比 $\mathbb{E}[(X-\mu)^2]$ 更省事。

Naive Bayes 因子分解（预告）
$$ P(Y\mid X_1,\dots,X_d) \;\propto\; P(Y)\prod_{i=1}^{d} P(X_i\mid Y) $$

符号. $Y$ 是类别标签；$X_i$ 是第 $i$ 个特征（共 $d$ 个）；$P(Y)$ 是类 prior（每个类别有多常见）；$P(X_i\mid Y)$ 是各特征的类条件 likelihood。"$\propto$" 隐藏了归一化常数 $P(X)$，它与 $y$ 无关，所以不影响 $\arg\max$。

怎么理解. 贝叶斯（分子）+ 条件独立假设（似然分解）。把参数数从 $\sim 2^d$ 压到 $2d$——NB 在高维可行的唯一原因。

怎么用. 训练靠在每个类别下数特征出现次数，估出 $P(Y)$ 与各 $P(X_i\mid Y)$；预测 $\hat y=\arg\max_y P(Y)\prod_i P(X_i\mid Y)$。代码里用 $\log$ 求和代替乘积避免下溢。

5. 例题精解

例 1 · 两骰子的条件概率（Lecture 2, p. 5–6）

掷两枚公平六面骰，记 $X_1, X_2$。求 $P(X_1=2 \mid X_1+X_2\le 4)$。

$X_1+X_2\le 4$ 的结果：$(1,1),(1,2),(1,3),(2,1),(2,2),(3,1)$ 共 6 个；其中 $X_1=2$ 的是 $(2,1),(2,2)$。

$$ P(X_1=2 \mid X_1+X_2\le 4)=\frac{2}{6}=\frac{1}{3}. $$
例 2 · 贝叶斯计算

已知 $P(A)=0.3,\ P(B\mid A)=0.8,\ P(B\mid \neg A)=0.2$，求 $P(A\mid B)$。

$$ P(B)=0.8(0.3)+0.2(0.7)=0.38 $$
$$ P(A\mid B)=\frac{0.8\cdot 0.3}{0.38}\approx 0.6316 $$

解读：原本只有 30% 信 $A$，看到 $B$ 后升到 63%——因为 $B$ 在 $A$ 下出现的概率比在 $\neg A$ 下高得多。

例 3 · 条件独立反例（Lecture 2, p. 9–10）

两骰子。$A=\{X_1\text{ 奇}\}$，$B=\{X_2\text{ 奇}\}$，$S=\{X_1+X_2\text{ 偶}\}$。无条件下 $A\perp B$（各 $\tfrac12$，联合 $\tfrac14$）。

但给定 $S$ 后二者相关：若 $A$ 成立，$B$ 必须成立才能让和为偶，所以 $P(B\mid A,S)=1\ne P(B\mid S)=\tfrac12$。

结论：独立 ≠ 条件独立（反之亦然），永远回到定义验证。

例 4 · 列联表估概率（Lecture 2, p. 14–17）

从肺癌玩具表估 $P(\text{Cancer}=Y\mid \text{Gender}=M,\ \text{Smoke}=Y)$。$M,Y,Y$ 行 1000；$M,Y$ 行（不论癌否）共 $1000+5000=6000$。$\hat P=1000/6000\approx 16.7\%$。

表小才能这么数。100 个二值特征的表会有 $2^{100}\approx 10^{30}$ 行，远超地球总人口；绝大多数格子是空的——这就是维度灾难（Lecture 2, p. 17–18）。

6. 题型与解题步骤

看到题怎么下手
  1. 识别题型：“A 与 B 同时”→联合，链式分解；“给 B 求 A”→条件，只有 $P(B\mid A)$ 时用贝叶斯；“是否独立？”→验 $P(A,B)=P(A)P(B)$。
  2. $P(B)$ 没给就用全概率公式展开。
  3. “给定 $C$，$A,B$ 是否独立？”列小表算 $P(A\mid B,C)$ 与 $P(A\mid C)$，相等即条件独立。
  4. ML 分类题：写 $P(Y\mid X)\propto P(Y)\prod_i P(X_i\mid Y)$，再 $\arg\max_y$。
  5. 极端情形自检：取 $P(B\mid A)=1$、$P(B\mid \neg A)=0$，公式给不出明显答案就是漏了项。

7. 常见错误

容易丢分的地方
  • 分母错：$P(A\mid B)=P(A,B)/P(B)$，不是 $P(A)$。
  • 把 $P(A\mid B)$ 与 $P(B\mid A)$ 当成同一回事（“检察官谬误”）；它们只通过先验相联系。
  • 把独立和条件独立混为一谈——例 3 是经典反例。
  • 贝叶斯里漏了先验（base-rate neglect）。
  • 用了 $\propto$ 但忘了最后归一化。
  • 变量并非条件独立时强行连乘——NB 的因子分解是假设，不是恒等式。

8. 考试重点

必须会的内容
  • 30 秒内默写五条核心公式：条件、贝叶斯、独立、条件独立、全概率。
  • 给 $P(A), P(B\mid A), P(B\mid \neg A)$ 手算 $P(A\mid B)$，含全概率展开过程。
  • 能在图模型 / NB 题里识别条件独立并写出因子分解。
  • 把 “posterior $\propto$ likelihood × prior” 落到具体变量上。
  • 能解释概率表为何在高维失效（$2^d$ 增长）。

9. 速记清单

最后一刻能默

  • $P(A\mid B)=P(A,B)/P(B)$，分母是被条件的事件。
  • 贝叶斯：$P(A\mid B)=P(B\mid A)P(A)/P(B)$。
  • $P(B)=\sum_a P(B\mid A=a)P(A=a)$，没给时自己展开。
  • 独立：$P(A,B)=P(A)P(B)$；条件独立：$P(A,B\mid C)=P(A\mid C)P(B\mid C)$。
  • 独立 ↮ 条件独立。
  • Naive Bayes：$P(Y\mid X)\propto P(Y)\prod_i P(X_i\mid Y)$，$2^d \to 2d$。
  • 概率落在 $[0,1]$，被条件变量上的条件概率求和为 1。
