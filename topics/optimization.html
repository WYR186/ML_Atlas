🧭 Optimization

1. Concept Understanding

Formal definitions and the problem they solve

Training a model = solving an optimization problem with the loss as objective. The unconstrained version (Lecture 3, p. 7) is $\min_w f(w)$; the constrained version adds inequalities $f_i(w)\le 0$ and equalities $h_j(w)=0$.

Three things matter:

  • Convexity: chord above the graph; equivalently, tangent below the graph; equivalently, $\nabla^2 f\succeq 0$. PD everywhere ⇒ strictly convex with a unique minimizer.
  • Gradient descent: iterate $w_{k+1}=w_k-\alpha_k\nabla f(w_k)$. For convex $f$, $\nabla f(w^*)=0$ is necessary and sufficient for global optimality (Lecture 3, p. 18).
  • Lagrangian / duality: turn constraints into penalties via $L(w,\lambda,\nu)=f_0(w)+\sum_i\lambda_if_i(w)+\sum_j\nu_jh_j(w)$ with $\lambda_i\ge 0$. The dual function $g(\lambda,\nu)=\min_w L$ gives a lower bound; under strong duality the bound is tight.

Convexity decides whether GD's stationary point is the right answer; the Lagrangian gives us SVM duals, kernel tricks, and the conceptual basis for primal-dual algorithms.

2. Plain-English Explanation

Without formulas first

Think of the parameters as a hiker's location and the loss as elevation. Optimization is "find the lowest point," and a convex function is a single bowl — drop a ball anywhere and it always rolls to the same bottom.

Gradient descent is just "look at the steepest downhill direction here, take a step, repeat." Step too big and you launch yourself across the valley; step too small and it takes forever.

Constraints are walls. The Lagrangian doesn't forbid you from crossing them — it charges $\lambda_i$ per unit of violation, turning the constrained problem back into ordinary unconstrained optimization.

Duality is looking at the same problem from the other side: instead of "minimize over locations," you "maximize over prices." For convex problems the two answers match, and sometimes the price-side problem is much easier.

3. Core Intuition

Why these ideas actually work

Convexity is the safety net. Without it, the solver may stop at a local minimum or saddle point that is much worse than the global one. With it, $\nabla f(w^*)=0$ alone is enough — no further checks needed (Lecture 3, p. 18).

The two convexity tests agree. The first-order test ("graph above every tangent") and the second-order test ("$\nabla^2 f\succeq 0$ everywhere") are equivalent, but the second is usually easier because you can read PSD off the structure (e.g. $X^\top BX$).

Gradient descent's direction is locally optimal: among unit vectors, $-\nabla f(w)$ maximizes the rate of decrease. Step size matters: too large diverges, too small crawls; constant $\alpha=1/L$ (Lipschitz constant $L$) is a textbook safe choice, and Armijo line search adapts.

The Lagrangian turns constraints into penalties. If $\hat w$ violates a constraint, $\max_\lambda L(\hat w,\lambda)=+\infty$, so the outer min refuses to choose it. Inside the feasible set, $\sum_i\lambda_if_i(w)\le 0$, so $L\le f_0$ — that is exactly where the dual lower bound comes from.

Why bother with the dual (Lecture 3, p. 31): sometimes fewer / simpler constraints, sometimes easier to optimize, and always provides a lower bound for the primal even when strong duality fails. The SVM dual depends only on inner products $x_i^\top x_j$, which is what enables the kernel trick.

4. Key Equations

Convexity (chord)
$$ f((1-\lambda)w_1+\lambda w_2)\le (1-\lambda)f(w_1)+\lambda f(w_2) $$

Symbols. $w_1, w_2$ are any two points in the (convex) domain; $\lambda\in[0,1]$ interpolates between them. Left side: $f$ at the interpolated point. Right side: same interpolation of the function values.

How to read it. The chord from $(w_1,f(w_1))$ to $(w_2,f(w_2))$ lies above the graph of $f$ between them — bowl-shaped curves never bulge above their chords.

How to use. The defining property of convexity. Most useful when $\nabla^2 f$ doesn't exist (norms, $|x|$): plug in the two points and the triangle / Jensen inequality usually closes the proof.

First-order test
$$ f(w_1)\ge f(w_2)+\nabla f(w_2)^\top(w_1-w_2) $$

Symbols. $\nabla f(w_2)$ is the gradient at the anchor point $w_2$. The right side is the tangent (first-order Taylor) plane at $w_2$ evaluated at $w_1$.

How to read it. The graph of a convex $f$ lies above every tangent hyperplane — there's no point you can stand at where the surface curves underneath you.

How to use. Holds for all $w_1, w_2$ ⇔ $f$ convex (when $f$ is differentiable). Often paired with $\nabla f(w^*)=0$ to conclude $f(w_1)\ge f(w^*)$ — that's the proof local = global for convex problems.

Second-order test
$$ \nabla^2 f(w)\succeq 0\ \forall w\ \Longleftrightarrow\ f \text{ convex} $$

Symbols. $\nabla^2 f(w)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ is the Hessian (symmetric). $\succeq 0$ means PSD: $v^\top \nabla^2 f(w)\,v\ge 0$ for all $v$. PD everywhere ⇒ strictly convex (unique minimizer if one exists).

How to read it. Curvature is non-negative in every direction → the surface bends upward everywhere → it's a bowl.

How to use. Compute the Hessian; show it factors as $X^\top D X$ with $D\succeq 0$ (sandwich identity), or show its eigenvalues are non-negative. The cleanest convexity proof for differentiable losses (logistic, ridge, log-sum-exp).

Optimality (convex)
$$ \nabla f(w^*)=0 \;\Rightarrow\; w^* \text{ globally optimal} $$

Symbols. $w^*$ is a stationary point (zero gradient). The implication holds only when $f$ is convex. For non-convex $f$, $\nabla f(w^*)=0$ is necessary but not sufficient — could be a max, saddle, or weak local min.

How to read it. A bowl has exactly one bottom (or a flat valley of equally good bottoms). Standing where the slope is zero on a bowl means standing at the lowest point.

How to use. If $f$ is convex, set $\nabla f=0$ and solve. For OLS that gives the normal equations $X^\top X w = X^\top y$. For non-convex $f$, you have to verify with a second-derivative test or rely on optimization-algorithm guarantees.

Gradient Descent
$$ w_{k+1}=w_k-\alpha_k\,\nabla f(w_k) $$

Symbols. $w_k$ is the iterate at step $k$. $\alpha_k>0$ is the step size (learning rate). $\nabla f(w_k)$ is the gradient at the current point — the direction of steepest ascent; the minus sign turns it into descent.

How to read it. Look at the slope at your feet, take a step downhill, repeat. Step too large and you overshoot the valley; too small and progress crawls.

How to use. Initialize $w_0$, iterate the update, stop when $\|\nabla f(w_k)\|\approx 0$ or after a budget. Common step sizes: constant $\alpha=1/L$ (where $L$ is the gradient's Lipschitz constant — guaranteed safe), diminishing $\alpha_k=1/k$, or Armijo line search.

Subgradient inequality
$$ f(x)\ge f(x_0)+\langle s, x-x_0\rangle,\quad s\in\partial f(x_0) $$

Symbols. $\partial f(x_0)$ is the subdifferential at $x_0$ — the set of all valid subgradients $s$. At a smooth point $\partial f(x_0)=\{\nabla f(x_0)\}$ (singleton); at a kink (like $|x|$ at 0) it's a whole interval, e.g. $[-1,1]$.

How to read it. Even at kinks, a convex function lies above some line through the point. The subdifferential is the set of slopes of all such supporting lines.

How to use. Replaces the first-order test for non-differentiable convex $f$. Subgradient descent: $x_{t+1}=x_t-\eta_t s_t$ for any $s_t\in\partial f(x_t)$. The inequality $f(x)-f(x^*)\le \langle s, x-x^*\rangle$ is the starting point for convergence proofs (HW1 §4.6).

Armijo line search
$$ f(w_k+\alpha d_k)-f(w_k)\le \sigma\alpha\,\nabla f(w_k)^\top d_k $$

Symbols. $d_k$ is the descent direction (often $-\nabla f(w_k)$). $\sigma\in(0,1)$ is a "fraction of expected decrease," typically 0.1 or 0.5. The right side is negative because $\nabla f(w_k)^\top d_k<0$ for descent directions.

How to read it. Demand that we get at least a fraction $\sigma$ of the linear-Taylor-predicted decrease. If we don't, the step was too big — shrink and retry.

How to use. Try $\alpha=s,\,\beta s,\,\beta^2 s,\dots$ (with backoff factor $\beta\in(0,1)$, e.g. 0.5) until the inequality is satisfied. Then take the step. This adapts step size automatically without needing to know $L$ (Lecture 3, p. 24).

Lagrangian
$$ L(w,\lambda,\nu)=f_0(w)+\sum_i\lambda_i f_i(w)+\sum_j\nu_j h_j(w),\ \lambda_i\ge 0 $$

Symbols. $f_0$ = objective. $f_i\le 0$ are inequality constraints with multipliers $\lambda_i\ge 0$. $h_j=0$ are equality constraints with free-sign multipliers $\nu_j$. The multipliers are the "prices" charged per unit of constraint violation.

How to read it. Don't forbid constraint violations — charge for them. If $\hat w$ violates $f_i\le 0$, the inner $\max$ pushes $\lambda_i\to+\infty$ and makes $L$ infinite, so the outer $\min$ refuses to choose $\hat w$.

How to use. Convert constrained problem $\min_w f_0$ s.t. $f_i\le 0,\ h_j=0$ into the unconstrained min-max $\min_w\max_{\lambda\ge 0,\nu}L$. KKT conditions ($\nabla_w L=0$, $\lambda_i f_i=0$, …) characterize the optimum.

Primal vs Dual
$$ \text{Primal: }\min_w\max_{\lambda\ge 0,\nu} L,\quad \text{Dual: }\max_{\lambda\ge 0,\nu}\min_w L $$

Symbols. Primal variables: the original $w$. Dual variables: the multipliers $\lambda,\nu$. Dual function $g(\lambda,\nu)=\min_w L(w,\lambda,\nu)$ — minimize $L$ in $w$ analytically, leaving a function of the multipliers only.

How to read it. Two views of the same problem: primal asks "where in $w$-space is the answer?", dual asks "what prices should we charge to make the constraints non-binding?".

How to use. Recipe (Lecture 3, p. 29): write $L$, minimize over $w$ to get $g$, read off any domain restrictions, solve $\max_{\lambda\ge 0,\nu} g$. SVM dual is special because it depends on the data only through inner products $x_i^\top x_j$ — that opens the door to the kernel trick.

Weak / Strong Duality
$$ f_0(w^*)\ge g(\lambda^*,\nu^*)\ (\text{always}),\quad =\ \text{under strong duality} $$

Symbols. $f_0(w^*)$ = optimal primal value. $g(\lambda^*,\nu^*)$ = optimal dual value. The gap $f_0(w^*)-g(\lambda^*,\nu^*)\ge 0$ is the duality gap.

How to read it. Game-theoretic: "whoever plays second has the advantage." On the LHS the inner $\max$ reacts to a chosen $w$; on the RHS the outer $\max$ commits first, so $\min_w\max_\lambda L\ge\max_\lambda\min_w L$.

How to use. Weak duality always holds and gives a lower bound on the primal. Strong duality (zero gap) typically requires convex primal + a constraint qualification like Slater's condition (a strictly feasible interior point exists). For convex problems you can just solve the dual instead — same answer.

5. Worked Examples

Example 1 · Logistic loss is convex (HW1 §4.1–4.2)

The Hessian factors as $H=X^\top BX$ with $B$ diagonal, $B_{ii}=\sigma(w^\top x_i)(1-\sigma(w^\top x_i))\in[0,\tfrac14]$. For any $v$, $v^\top H v=(Xv)^\top B(Xv)=\sum_i B_{ii}(Xv)_i^2\ge 0$, so $H\succeq 0$. By the second-order test the loss is convex everywhere; every local minimum is global.

Example 2 · $\|x\|_2$ is convex (HW1 §4.3)

By the triangle inequality, $\|(1-\lambda)x_1+\lambda x_2\|\le (1-\lambda)\|x_1\|+\lambda\|x_2\|$ — the chord definition. Same argument works for any norm. The Hessian fails to exist at $x=0$, so the chord definition is the cleanest path.

Example 3 · Log-sum-exp is convex (HW1 §4.4, Lecture 3 p. 16)

Let $f(x)=\log\sum_i\exp(x_i)$. Then $\partial f/\partial x_i=p_i$ where $p$ is the softmax of $x$, and $\nabla^2 f=\mathrm{diag}(p)-pp^\top$. By Cauchy-Schwarz, $\langle v,p\rangle^2\le\sum_i p_iv_i^2$, so $v^\top(\mathrm{diag}(p)-pp^\top)v\ge 0$ and $f$ is convex.

Example 4 · Lagrangian for a Linear Program (Lecture 3, p. 30)

$\min_w c^\top w$ s.t. $Aw\le b$. Then $L(w,\lambda)=(c+A^\top\lambda)^\top w-b^\top\lambda$ with $\lambda\ge 0$.

Minimizing over $w$ gives $-b^\top\lambda$ if $c+A^\top\lambda=0$, otherwise $-\infty$. The dual is therefore $\max_{\lambda\ge 0}\,-b^\top\lambda$ s.t. $A^\top\lambda+c=0$ — also an LP, so we can pick whichever is easier.

Example 5 · Duality T/F (HW1 §4.5)

(a) Feasible set $\{x:g(x)\le 0\}$ for convex $g$ is convex — True, sublevel sets of convex functions are convex.

(b) The dual function is always concave — True, it is a pointwise infimum of affine functions of $(\lambda,\nu)$.

(c) Strong duality ⇒ primal value = dual value — True, by definition.

(d) The dual always has the same solution as the primal — False: only the optimal values match (under strong duality); the variables live in different spaces (Lecture 3, p. 33).

6. Problem-Solving Tips

How to tackle an optimization problem
  1. "Is $f$ convex?" Try second-order ($\nabla^2 f\succeq 0$) first; for non-smooth $f$, fall back to the chord or first-order definition.
  2. "Find the optimum." Convex + differentiable: solve $\nabla f(w)=0$. Quadratic: invert the Hessian. Otherwise: gradient descent.
  3. "Show local = global." Cite convexity + second-order test.
  4. "Constrained problem." Write the Lagrangian with $\lambda_i\ge 0$ on inequalities, $\nu_j$ free on equalities; then either KKT (closed form) or take the dual.
  5. "Compute the dual" (Lecture 3 recipe, p. 29): write $L$, minimize over $w$ with $\lambda,\nu$ fixed, read off domain restrictions, write the dual as $\max g(\lambda,\nu)$.
  6. Subgradient question. Use $f(x)\ge f(x_0)+\langle s, x-x_0\rangle$, $s\in\partial f(x_0)$; e.g. $\partial|x|(0)=[-1,1]$.
  7. GD sanity check: if loss diverges, halve $\alpha$; if it crawls, double it. With Lipschitz $L$, $\alpha=1/L$ is a default that always works.

7. Common Mistakes

Frequent traps
  • Confusing PSD and PD — PSD allows zero eigenvalues, so the optimum need not be unique.
  • Treating $\nabla f(w^*)=0$ as sufficient for non-convex $f$ — it only marks a stationary point.
  • Step size too large — GD can diverge spectacularly; always check the loss decreases.
  • Forgetting $\lambda_i\ge 0$ on inequality multipliers (equality multipliers $\nu_j$ are free).
  • Confusing primal and dual variables — they live in different spaces; only optimal values match.
  • Asserting strong duality without justification — typically needs convexity + Slater.
  • Using ordinary gradient on a non-differentiable $f$ (e.g. $|x|$ at 0) — use a subgradient.

8. Exam Focus

What you must walk in able to do
  • State the chord, first-order, and second-order definitions of convexity, and switch between them.
  • Use $\nabla^2 f\succeq 0$ to prove logistic, log-sum-exp, ridge, etc. convex.
  • Recognize $X^\top BX\succeq 0$ via the sandwich trick.
  • Write one step of gradient descent and explain step-size trade-offs.
  • Set up the Lagrangian for a constrained problem, then derive the dual via the four-step recipe.
  • State weak duality and distinguish from strong duality.
  • Know the four T/F items in HW1 §4.5 — they recur on quizzes.

9. Quick Checklist

Last-minute recall

  • Convex ⇔ chord above graph ⇔ tangent below graph ⇔ Hessian PSD.
  • Convex $f$: $\nabla f(w^*)=0\Rightarrow$ global minimum.
  • GD: $w_{k+1}=w_k-\alpha\nabla f(w_k)$; $\alpha=1/L$ safe.
  • $H=X^\top BX\succeq 0$ when $B\succeq 0$ — logistic loss convex.
  • Norms, log-sum-exp, exp, non-negative weighted sums of convex are convex.
  • Lagrangian: $L=f_0+\sum\lambda_if_i+\sum\nu_jh_j$, $\lambda_i\ge 0$.
  • Dual $g(\lambda,\nu)=\min_w L$; problem $\max_{\lambda\ge 0,\nu} g$.
  • Weak duality: $\min\max L\ge\max\min L$ (always).
  • Strong duality: equality, typically convex + Slater.
  • Subgradient: $f(x)\ge f(x_0)+\langle s,x-x_0\rangle$, $s\in\partial f(x_0)$.

🧭 优化

1. 概念理解

正式定义与它解决的问题

训练模型 = 解“以损失为目标”的优化问题。无约束版本（Lecture 3, p. 7）是 $\min_w f(w)$；有约束版本再加上 $f_i(w)\le 0$ 与 $h_j(w)=0$。

真正要掌握三件事：

  • 凸性：弦在图像上方，等价于切线在图像下方，等价于 $\nabla^2 f\succeq 0$。处处 PD ⇒ 严格凸、唯一极小。
  • 梯度下降：$w_{k+1}=w_k-\alpha_k\nabla f(w_k)$。凸 $f$ 下，$\nabla f(w^*)=0$ 既必要也充分（Lecture 3, p. 18）。
  • Lagrangian / 对偶：把约束化为罚金，$L(w,\lambda,\nu)=f_0+\sum_i\lambda_if_i+\sum_j\nu_jh_j$，$\lambda_i\ge 0$。对偶函数 $g(\lambda,\nu)=\min_w L$ 给出下界；强对偶时下界取等。

凸性决定了 GD 找到的驻点是不是真答案；Lagrangian 给我们 SVM 对偶、核技巧，以及原始-对偶算法的概念基础。

2. 大白话理解

不看公式先懂大概

把参数想成登山者位置，损失就是海拔。优化就是“找最低点”，而凸函数是个单一碗——任意位置放球都会滚到同一个底。

梯度下降就是“看哪个方向最陡，迈一步，重复”。步长太大会把自己甩出山谷，太小爬蜗牛。

约束就是墙。Lagrangian 不禁止你越界，而是对每单位违规收 $\lambda_i$，把有约束问题变回普通的无约束问题。

对偶就是从另一面看同一题：与其“在位置上取最小”，不如“在价格上取最大”。凸问题下两个答案一致，且价格那一面有时简单很多。

3. 核心直觉

这些概念为何有效

凸性是安全网。没有它，优化器可能停在比全局还差很多的局部 / 鞍点；有它，$\nabla f(w^*)=0$ 就够了，不需要再补条件（Lecture 3, p. 18）。

判凸的两条等价路径：一阶（图像在每条切平面上方）与二阶（$\nabla^2 f\succeq 0$）等价；二阶通常更好用，因为可以从结构（如 $X^\top BX$）直接读出 PSD。

梯度下降的方向是局部最优：单位向量中 $-\nabla f(w)$ 让一阶 Taylor 下降率最大。步长很关键：太大发散，太小爬慢；常数 $\alpha=1/L$（Lipschitz $L$）是教科书安全选择，Armijo 线搜索自适应。

Lagrangian 把约束变成罚金。若 $\hat w$ 违约，$\max_\lambda L(\hat w,\lambda)=+\infty$，外层 min 就不会选它；可行域内 $\sum_i\lambda_if_i(w)\le 0$，所以 $L\le f_0$——对偶下界由此而来。

为什么要做对偶（Lecture 3, p. 31）：约束更少 / 更简、有时更易解、永远提供原始的下界（即便强对偶不成立）。SVM 对偶只依赖内积 $x_i^\top x_j$，这正是核技巧的源头。

4. 关键公式

凸性（弦）
$$ f((1-\lambda)w_1+\lambda w_2)\le (1-\lambda)f(w_1)+\lambda f(w_2) $$

符号. $w_1, w_2$ 是定义域（凸集）里任意两点，$\lambda\in[0,1]$ 在它们间插值。左边：$f$ 在插值点的值；右边：函数值的同样插值。

怎么理解. 从 $(w_1,f(w_1))$ 到 $(w_2,f(w_2))$ 的弦永远在 $f$ 图像上方——碗形曲线不会鼓出弦之外。

怎么用. 凸性的定义。最有用的场合是 $\nabla^2 f$ 不存在时（范数、$|x|$）：代两点用三角 / Jensen 不等式即可收尾。

一阶判定
$$ f(w_1)\ge f(w_2)+\nabla f(w_2)^\top(w_1-w_2) $$

符号. $\nabla f(w_2)$ 是锚点 $w_2$ 处的梯度；右边是 $w_2$ 处一阶 Taylor 切超平面在 $w_1$ 的值。

怎么理解. 凸 $f$ 的图像永远在每条切超平面上方——找不到一个站着会被曲面"包住"的点。

怎么用. 对所有 $w_1, w_2$ 成立 ⇔ $f$ 凸（可微情形）。常和 $\nabla f(w^*)=0$ 配合得 $f(w_1)\ge f(w^*)$——这就是凸问题"局部 = 全局"的证明。

二阶判定
$$ \nabla^2 f(w)\succeq 0\ \forall w\ \Longleftrightarrow\ f \text{ 凸} $$

符号. $\nabla^2 f(w)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ 是 Hessian（对称）；$\succeq 0$ 即 PSD：$v^\top \nabla^2 f(w)\,v\ge 0$ 对任意 $v$ 成立。处处 PD ⇒ 严格凸（若有最小值则唯一）。

怎么理解. 各方向曲率都 $\ge 0$ → 曲面处处向上弯 → 是个碗。

怎么用. 算 Hessian，凑成 $X^\top D X$（$D\succeq 0$）用夹心恒等式，或证特征值非负。可微损失（logistic、ridge、log-sum-exp）的最干净凸性证法。

凸优化的最优性
$$ \nabla f(w^*)=0 \;\Rightarrow\; w^* \text{ 全局最优} $$

符号. $w^*$ 是驻点（梯度为 0）。这条蕴含只在 $f$ 凸时成立；非凸时 $\nabla f(w^*)=0$ 必要但不充分——可能是极大、鞍点或弱局部极小。

怎么理解. 碗只有一个底（或一条等优的平谷）。在碗上找到斜率为 0 的点 = 最低点。

怎么用. 凸 $f$ 时令 $\nabla f=0$ 直接解。OLS 给出 normal equations $X^\top X w = X^\top y$。非凸时要二阶补判或靠优化算法的保证。

梯度下降
$$ w_{k+1}=w_k-\alpha_k\,\nabla f(w_k) $$

符号. $w_k$ 第 $k$ 步的迭代；$\alpha_k>0$ 步长（learning rate）；$\nabla f(w_k)$ 当前点的梯度——最陡上升方向，前面的负号让它变成下降。

怎么理解. 看脚下的坡度，朝下坡走一步，重复。步太大会冲出山谷；太小爬蜗牛。

怎么用. 初始化 $w_0$，反复迭代，$\|\nabla f(w_k)\|\approx 0$ 或达预算时停。常用步长：常数 $\alpha=1/L$（$L$ 为梯度的 Lipschitz 常数，安全）；递减 $\alpha_k=1/k$；或 Armijo 线搜索。

次梯度不等式
$$ f(x)\ge f(x_0)+\langle s, x-x_0\rangle,\quad s\in\partial f(x_0) $$

符号. $\partial f(x_0)$ 是 subdifferential——$x_0$ 处所有合法 subgradient $s$ 的集合。光滑点 $\partial f(x_0)=\{\nabla f(x_0)\}$；尖点（如 $|x|$ 在 0）是整个区间，比如 $[-1,1]$。

怎么理解. 即使在尖点，凸函数也躺在某条过该点的直线上方；subdifferential 就是所有这种支撑直线斜率的集合。

怎么用. 替代不可微凸 $f$ 的一阶判定。Subgradient descent：$x_{t+1}=x_t-\eta_t s_t$（$s_t\in\partial f(x_t)$）。$f(x)-f(x^*)\le \langle s, x-x^*\rangle$ 是收敛性证明的起点（HW1 §4.6）。

Armijo 线搜索
$$ f(w_k+\alpha d_k)-f(w_k)\le \sigma\alpha\,\nabla f(w_k)^\top d_k $$

符号. $d_k$ 下降方向（常取 $-\nabla f(w_k)$）；$\sigma\in(0,1)$ 是"期望下降的占比"，常取 0.1 或 0.5。右侧为负——下降方向下 $\nabla f(w_k)^\top d_k<0$。

怎么理解. 要求至少拿到一阶 Taylor 预测的下降量的 $\sigma$ 倍。拿不到说明步太大——缩小再试。

怎么用. 尝试 $\alpha=s,\,\beta s,\,\beta^2 s,\dots$（回退因子 $\beta\in(0,1)$，常 0.5）直到不等式成立，再走这一步。无需知道 $L$ 即可自动适配步长（Lecture 3, p. 24）。

Lagrangian
$$ L(w,\lambda,\nu)=f_0(w)+\sum_i\lambda_i f_i(w)+\sum_j\nu_j h_j(w),\ \lambda_i\ge 0 $$

符号. $f_0$ 目标；$f_i\le 0$ 不等式约束、对应乘子 $\lambda_i\ge 0$；$h_j=0$ 等式约束、对应乘子 $\nu_j$ 自由号；乘子是"违约金"。

怎么理解. 不禁止违约，而是收费。$\hat w$ 违 $f_i\le 0$ 时内层 $\max$ 把 $\lambda_i\to+\infty$，$L$ 变正无穷，外层 $\min$ 自然不会选它。

怎么用. 把有约束 $\min_w f_0$ s.t. $f_i\le 0,\ h_j=0$ 化为无约束 $\min_w\max_{\lambda\ge 0,\nu}L$。KKT 条件（$\nabla_w L=0$、$\lambda_i f_i=0$、…）刻画最优。

原始 vs 对偶
$$ \text{Primal: }\min_w\max_{\lambda\ge 0,\nu} L,\quad \text{Dual: }\max_{\lambda\ge 0,\nu}\min_w L $$

符号. 原始变量：原来的 $w$；对偶变量：乘子 $\lambda,\nu$。对偶函数 $g(\lambda,\nu)=\min_w L(w,\lambda,\nu)$——把 $L$ 对 $w$ 解析地最小化掉，剩下只是乘子的函数。

怎么理解. 同一个问题的两个视角：原始问"答案在 $w$-空间的哪里"，对偶问"该收什么样的价让约束变松"。

怎么用. 配方（Lecture 3, p. 29）：写 $L$ → 对 $w$ 取 min 得 $g$ → 读出域约束 → 解 $\max_{\lambda\ge 0,\nu} g$。SVM 对偶之所以特别，是因为它对数据的依赖只有内积 $x_i^\top x_j$——这就打开了 kernel trick 的大门。

弱 / 强对偶
$$ f_0(w^*)\ge g(\lambda^*,\nu^*)\ (\text{恒成立}),\quad =\ \text{当强对偶成立时} $$

符号. $f_0(w^*)$ 原始最优值；$g(\lambda^*,\nu^*)$ 对偶最优值；二者之差 $\ge 0$ 称为对偶间隙。

怎么理解. 博弈论解读："后手有优势"。LHS 内层 $\max$ 看到 $w$ 后再选；RHS 外层 $\max$ 先承诺，所以 $\min_w\max_\lambda L\ge\max_\lambda\min_w L$。

怎么用. 弱对偶恒成立，给原始一个下界。强对偶（间隙为 0）通常需要原始凸 + 一条约束限定，如 Slater 条件（存在严格可行的内点）。凸问题下可以解对偶代替原始——答案相同。

5. 例题精解

例 1 · Logistic 损失为凸（HW1 §4.1–4.2）

Hessian 写成 $H=X^\top BX$，$B$ 对角且 $B_{ii}=\sigma(w^\top x_i)(1-\sigma(w^\top x_i))\in[0,\tfrac14]$。对任意 $v$，$v^\top H v=(Xv)^\top B(Xv)=\sum_i B_{ii}(Xv)_i^2\ge 0$，所以 $H\succeq 0$。二阶判定 ⇒ 损失处处凸；任意局部最优都是全局。

例 2 · $\|x\|_2$ 凸（HW1 §4.3）

三角不等式给出 $\|(1-\lambda)x_1+\lambda x_2\|\le (1-\lambda)\|x_1\|+\lambda\|x_2\|$ —— 这就是弦定义。任意范数都成立。$x=0$ 处 Hessian 不存在，所以弦定义是最干净的路径。

例 3 · Log-sum-exp 凸（HW1 §4.4，Lecture 3 p. 16）

$f(x)=\log\sum_i\exp(x_i)$。$\partial f/\partial x_i=p_i$（$p$ 为 $x$ 的 softmax），$\nabla^2 f=\mathrm{diag}(p)-pp^\top$。由 Cauchy-Schwarz，$\langle v,p\rangle^2\le\sum_i p_iv_i^2$，故 $v^\top(\mathrm{diag}(p)-pp^\top)v\ge 0$，$f$ 凸。

例 4 · LP 的 Lagrangian（Lecture 3, p. 30）

$\min_w c^\top w$ s.t. $Aw\le b$。$L(w,\lambda)=(c+A^\top\lambda)^\top w-b^\top\lambda$，$\lambda\ge 0$。

对 $w$ 取 min：$c+A^\top\lambda=0$ 时为 $-b^\top\lambda$，否则为 $-\infty$。对偶：$\max_{\lambda\ge 0}\,-b^\top\lambda$ s.t. $A^\top\lambda+c=0$——也是 LP，可挑更易解的那边。

例 5 · 对偶判断题（HW1 §4.5）

(a) $g$ 凸时可行域 $\{x:g(x)\le 0\}$ 凸——对，凸函数下水平集是凸集。

(b) 对偶函数总是凹——对，它是 $(\lambda,\nu)$ 仿射函数族的逐点下确界。

(c) 强对偶 ⇒ 原始最优值 = 对偶最优值——对，这就是定义。

(d) 对偶问题总是与原始有相同的解——错：只有最优值相等（强对偶下），变量在不同空间（Lecture 3, p. 33）。

6. 题型与解题步骤

看到题怎么下手
  1. “$f$ 凸吗？”先用二阶（$\nabla^2 f\succeq 0$）；不可微就回到弦或一阶定义。
  2. “求最优”：凸 + 可微 ⇒ $\nabla f(w)=0$；二次型直接求逆 Hessian；否则梯度下降。
  3. “证局部 = 全局”：引用凸 + 二阶判定。
  4. “有约束”：写 Lagrangian（$\lambda_i\ge 0$ 给不等式，$\nu_j$ 自由给等式）；之后 KKT 或对偶化。
  5. “求对偶”（Lecture 3 配方，p. 29）：写 $L$ → 把 $\lambda,\nu$ 当常数对 $w$ 取 min → 域限制 ⇒ 对偶约束 → 对偶为 $\max g$。
  6. “次梯度题”：$f(x)\ge f(x_0)+\langle s,x-x_0\rangle$，$s\in\partial f(x_0)$；$\partial|x|(0)=[-1,1]$。
  7. GD 自检：发散就把 $\alpha$ 减半；爬得慢就放大；Lipschitz $L$ 已知时 $\alpha=1/L$ 默认安全。

7. 常见错误

高频陷阱
  • PSD 与 PD 混淆——PSD 允许 0 特征值，最优可能不唯一。
  • 非凸时把 $\nabla f(w^*)=0$ 当充分——它只标驻点。
  • 步长过大——GD 可能爆，每步检查损失是否下降。
  • 忘了 $\lambda_i\ge 0$（等式乘子 $\nu_j$ 自由）。
  • 原始变量与对偶变量混为一谈——只有最优值相等。
  • 无凭据声称强对偶——通常需要凸 + Slater。
  • 对不可微 $f$（如 $|x|$ 在 0）用普通梯度——要用次梯度。

8. 考试重点

必须会的内容
  • 能默写并互换凸的三种定义（弦 / 一阶 / 二阶）。
  • 用 $\nabla^2 f\succeq 0$ 证 logistic、log-sum-exp、岭等的凸性。
  • 用夹心法识别 $X^\top BX\succeq 0$（$B\succeq 0$）。
  • 写一步 GD 并解释步长权衡。
  • 会写有约束问题的 Lagrangian，并按四步配方推导对偶。
  • 会陈述弱对偶并区分强对偶。
  • HW1 §4.5 那四条判断题反复出现，每条都要会论证。

9. 速记清单

最后一刻能默

  • 凸 ⇔ 弦在图像上方 ⇔ 切线在图像下方 ⇔ Hessian PSD。
  • 凸 $f$：$\nabla f(w^*)=0\Rightarrow$ 全局最优。
  • GD：$w_{k+1}=w_k-\alpha\nabla f(w_k)$，$\alpha=1/L$ 安全。
  • $H=X^\top BX\succeq 0$（$B\succeq 0$）—— logistic 损失凸。
  • 范数、log-sum-exp、指数、凸函数的非负加权和都是凸。
  • Lagrangian：$L=f_0+\sum\lambda_if_i+\sum\nu_jh_j$，$\lambda_i\ge 0$。
  • 对偶 $g(\lambda,\nu)=\min_w L$；$\max_{\lambda\ge 0,\nu} g$。
  • 弱对偶：$\min\max L\ge\max\min L$（恒成立）。
  • 强对偶：等号（凸 + Slater 通常成立）。
  • 次梯度：$f(x)\ge f(x_0)+\langle s,x-x_0\rangle$，$s\in\partial f(x_0)$。
