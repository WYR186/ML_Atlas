🟧 KNN — K-Nearest Neighbors

🧠 Intuition

"Nearby points carry similar labels." Supervised, non-parametric, can produce highly nonlinear decision boundaries.

Prediction rules

Classification (majority vote)
$$ \hat{y}(x)=\mathrm{mode}\{y_i:\ x_i \in \mathcal{N}_k(x)\} $$

Symbols. $\hat{y}(x)$: predicted label for query point $x$； $x$: query / test input we want to classify； $k$: number of neighbors to consider (hyperparameter)； $\mathcal{N}_k(x)$: set of the $k$ training points closest to $x$； $y_i$: known label of training point $x_i$； $\mathrm{mode}$: majority vote — the most-frequent label in the set

How to read it. KNN is a 'lazy learner' — there is no training, only a lookup. The prediction asks 'what do my $k$ closest historical neighbors say?' and goes with the majority. The whole method assumes that nearby points share labels, so accuracy depends entirely on whether the distance metric makes sense for your features.

How to use. For each test point $x$, compute its distance to every training sample, sort and pick the $k$ smallest; the most-frequent label among those $k$ neighbors is the prediction. Choose $k$ via cross-validation — small $k$ overfits noise, large $k$ over-smooths the boundary.

Regression (mean)
$$ \hat{y}(x)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{x_i\in \mathcal{N}_k(x)} y_i $$

Symbols. $\hat{y}(x)$: predicted real-valued target for $x$； $y_i \in \mathbb{R}$: real-valued target of training point $x_i$； $\mathcal{N}_k(x)$: the $k$ nearest training points to $x$ (same as classification)； $\frac{1}{k}\sum$: sum the $k$ neighbor targets, then divide by $k$ — the average

How to read it. Regression KNN is just a local average. The prediction is 'the typical target value seen near $x$.' It works well when the underlying function is roughly continuous and you have enough samples to densely cover the input space.

How to use. Same neighbor-finding step as classification, but instead of voting, average the neighbors' target values. A common variant weights each neighbor by $1/d(x, x_i)$ so closer neighbors influence the prediction more.

Euclidean distance
$$ d(x,x_i)=\|x-x_i\|_2=\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{d} (x_j-x_{ij})^2} $$

Symbols. $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$: query point with $d$ feature components $x_1, \dots, x_d$； $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$: the $i$-th training point with components $x_{i1}, \dots, x_{id}$； $j$: feature index — the sum runs over all $d$ features； $\|\cdot\|_2$: L2 (Euclidean) norm

How to read it. Geometric 'ruler distance' — the straight-line length between two points in $d$-dimensional space. The squared per-coordinate gap means large per-feature differences hurt much more than small ones, which is why feature scale matters so much.

How to use. Compute the squared difference per feature, sum across features, take the square root. Standardize features first (subtract mean, divide by std) — otherwise a feature on a larger numerical scale (e.g. salary in dollars vs age in years) dominates the distance. Common alternatives: Manhattan ($L_1$), cosine, Mahalanobis.

⚠️ Pitfall 1 · Feature scaling

KNN is distance-based, so a feature with a large numerical range dominates. Standardize (zero mean, unit variance) or normalize to [0, 1] before training.

⚠️ Pitfall 2 · Curse of dimensionality

In high dimensions the volume grows exponentially with d, so a fixed number of "neighbors" must come from a much larger radius. Distance concentration: the nearest and farthest points get arbitrarily close in relative distance.

✅ Exam focus
  • Numerical KNN: list distances, sort, take top-k, vote — handle ties per the problem's rule.
  • Concept: explain why KNN degrades in high dimensions.

🟧 KNN · K-Nearest Neighbors

🧠 直觉 / Intuition

“相似样本应有相似标签” — Similar points have similar labels.

监督 / Supervised · 非参数 / Non-parametric · 可产生非线性边界。

预测规则 / Prediction rule

分类 · Majority Vote
$$ \hat{y}(x)=\mathrm{mode}\{y_i:\ x_i \in \mathcal{N}_k(x)\} $$

符号. $\hat{y}(x)$: 对查询点 $x$ 的预测标签；$x$: 待分类的查询样本；$k$: 考虑的最近邻个数（超参数）；$\mathcal{N}_k(x)$: 训练集中距 $x$ 最近的 $k$ 个样本；$y_i$: 训练点 $x_i$ 的已知标签；$\mathrm{mode}$: 众数 —— 集合中出现最多的标签

怎么理解. KNN 是 lazy learner —— 没有训练，只是查表。预测就是问 "离我最近的 $k$ 个历史样本是什么类别？" 然后投票决定。它假设 "近邻点应该有相似标签"，所以效果完全取决于距离度量是否对你的特征有意义。

怎么用. 对每个测试点 $x$，计算它到所有训练样本的距离，排序取最小的 $k$ 个；这 $k$ 个邻居中出现最多的标签就是预测结果。$k$ 用 cross-validation 选 —— $k$ 太小会过拟合噪声，$k$ 太大会把决策边界过度平滑。

回归 · Average
$$ \hat{y}(x)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{x_i\in \mathcal{N}_k(x)} y_i $$

符号. $\hat{y}(x)$: 对 $x$ 的实数预测值；$y_i \in \mathbb{R}$: 训练点 $x_i$ 的实数目标值；$\mathcal{N}_k(x)$: $x$ 的 $k$ 个最近训练点（与分类相同）；$\frac{1}{k}\sum$: 对 $k$ 个邻居的目标值求和后除以 $k$ —— 即平均

怎么理解. 回归型 KNN 就是局部平均：预测值是 "$x$ 附近样本目标值的代表"。当真实函数大致连续、训练样本能稠密覆盖输入空间时效果好。

怎么用. 找邻居的步骤与分类一致；但不再投票，而是把 $k$ 个邻居的目标值取平均。常见变体是用 $1/d(x, x_i)$ 加权，让更近的邻居权重更大。

Euclidean Distance
$$ d(x,x_i)=\|x-x_i\|_2=\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{d}(x_j-x_{ij})^2} $$

符号. $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$: $d$ 维查询点，分量为 $x_1, \dots, x_d$；$x_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$: 第 $i$ 个训练点，分量为 $x_{i1}, \dots, x_{id}$；$j$: 特征下标 —— 对全部 $d$ 个特征求和；$\|\cdot\|_2$: L2 范数（欧氏范数）

怎么理解. 几何上的 "直尺距离" —— $d$ 维空间中两点的直线长度。每个维度差的平方把大差异放大、小差异变小，所以特征尺度对它影响很大。

怎么用. 每个特征算差的平方，对所有特征求和后开方。先做特征标准化（减均值、除标准差） —— 否则数值范围大的特征（如以美元计的工资 vs. 以年计的年龄）会主导距离。常见替代：Manhattan ($L_1$)、cosine、Mahalanobis。

⚠️ 高频考点 1 · Feature Scaling

KNN 基于距离，数值范围更大的特征会“主导”距离 → 必须标准化（均值 0 方差 1）或归一化（0–1）。

⚠️ 高频考点 2 · Curse of Dimensionality

维度升高时空间体积指数增长，要找到足够邻居就要扩到很大半径，邻居不再“局部”。常考表述：高维下最近点与最远点距离差别变小（distance concentration），最近邻概念变弱。

✅ Exam focus / 解题技巧
  • 计算题：先列距离表，排序选前 $k$ 个；遇平票按题目规则。
  • 概念题：高维 KNN 失效的统计直觉（distance concentration）。
